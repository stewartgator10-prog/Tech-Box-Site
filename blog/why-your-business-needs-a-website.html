Why a Website Is One of the Best Investments Your Business Can Make

By Tech Box Solutions · July 27, 2026 · 6 min read

Here's an uncomfortable truth for a lot of small business owners: most of your future customers will size you up online before they ever pick up the phone. They'll search your name, glance at what comes up, and decide in a few seconds whether you look like a real, trustworthy business — or whether they should move on to the next result.

If you don't have a website, or the one you have is slow, dated, or hard to use on a phone, you're losing those people quietly. You never get the call, so you never even know it happened. Let's look at what the numbers actually say — and why a quality website is one of the highest-return investments a small business can make.

1. People check you out online before they buy

Shopping today starts with a search, even for local, in-person services. Roughly 81% of shoppers research a business online before making a purchase, and a large majority visit a company's website before ever walking through the door.[1]

Social media alone doesn't close that gap. About 84% of consumers consider a business with its own website more credible than one that only has social media pages.[1] A Facebook page is fine, but it's rented land — a website is the storefront you actually own, and customers know the difference.

2. Your website is your first impression — and it's mostly visual

First impressions online form almost instantly, and they're overwhelmingly about design. Studies have found that 94% of first impressions of a website are design-related, and that 75% of people judge a company's credibility based on its website design.[1]

Think about what that means. Before a visitor reads a single word about your pricing or your experience, the look and feel of your site has already told them whether you're worth taking seriously. A clean, professional, fast site makes a one-person operation look established. A broken or dated one makes even a great business look risky.

3. No website means you're invisible in local search

This is the big one for a local business in a place like Frederick. Nearly half of all Google searches — about 46% — have local intent, meaning people are looking for a business, product, or service near them.[2] And local searches convert fast: 76% of people who search for something nearby on their phone visit a related business within 24 hours.[2]

Those "plumber near me" and "web designer in Frederick" searches are people ready to act today. But to show up for them, Google needs a real website to point to — one it can read, trust, and connect to your service area. No site, no listing to rank, no spot in that race.

76%
of people who run a "near me" search visit a business within a day.[2]

4. A slow or clumsy site quietly costs you customers

Having a website isn't enough on its own — it has to be fast and work well on a phone, because that's where most people will find you. According to Google, 53% of mobile visitors abandon a site that takes longer than three seconds to load.[3] Around 40% will leave a business's site entirely if it's a poor experience on mobile.[1]

Every one of those bounces is a potential customer who was interested enough to click — and left before you got a chance. This is exactly why we build sites to load quickly and look sharp on every screen, not just a desktop monitor.

5. Websites drive real, measurable growth

This isn't just about looking professional — it shows up on the bottom line. A Google and Deloitte study of more than 4,500 small businesses found that companies with a strong online presence were 2.8 times more likely to grow their revenue than those without one.[4]

And here's the opportunity: roughly 27% of U.S. small businesses still don't have a website at all.[1] In a competitive local market, that gap is an opening. A quality website doesn't just keep you in the game — it puts you ahead of a real slice of your competition that hasn't shown up yet.

The bottom line

A website is no longer a "nice to have." It's the first handshake, the storefront that's open at 2 a.m., and the single thing that decides whether a searcher becomes a customer or a missed opportunity. For the cost, few investments do more work for a small business — as long as it's built well, loads fast, and is easy to use on a phone.
