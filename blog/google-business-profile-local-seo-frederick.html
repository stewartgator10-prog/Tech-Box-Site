Google Business Profile: How Frederick, MD Businesses Get Found on Google

By Tech Box Solutions · September 7, 2026 · 5 min read

Before a customer in Frederick ever lands on your website, there's a good chance they've already seen your Google Business Profile — that box with your hours, your reviews, your photos, and a "Directions" button that shows up when someone searches your name or "plumber near me." It's free to set up. It's often the very first impression you make. And a lot of small businesses either haven't claimed it, or claimed it once and never touched it again.

That's a problem, because this one free listing does more heavy lifting than most owners realize. Let's break down what it actually is, why it matters so much for a local business, and the handful of fixes that make the biggest difference.

1. Your Google Business Profile is your storefront on the map

When someone searches "HVAC repair Frederick MD" or "bakery near me," Google doesn't just show a list of blue links anymore. It shows a map with a handful of local businesses pinned, along with their rating, hours, and a call button — right at the top of the page, before any website even appears. That panel is your Google Business Profile (formerly known as Google My Business), and for a huge share of local searches, it's the only thing a customer sees before deciding whether to call.

If your profile is missing, unclaimed, or has the wrong hours and no photos, you're either invisible in that map pack or you're actively talking a ready-to-buy customer out of choosing you — while a competitor with a filled-out profile picks up the call instead.

2. Reviews are doing more of the deciding than ever

Your Google Business Profile is also where most people read your reviews before they read anything else about you. According to BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, and the bar keeps rising — 41% now say they "always" read reviews before choosing a local business, up from 29% just a year earlier.[1]

That means your star rating and how you respond to reviews aren't a side project — they're one of the main things standing between a search and a phone call. A profile with a handful of stale, unanswered reviews reads as neglect, even if the work you actually do is excellent.

3. Local searches are enormous — and they convert fast

This isn't a niche channel. Google itself has said that roughly 46% of all searches carry local intent — meaning close to half of everything typed into Google is someone looking for a business, product, or service near them, not just general information.[2] For a plumber, a salon, a landscaper, or a retail shop in Frederick County, that's an enormous, constantly-running audience of people who are ready to act, not just browsing.

4. The fixes that actually move the needle

You don't need to be a marketing expert to get real value out of your profile. The businesses that show up consistently tend to do a handful of simple things well:

  • Claim and verify it. If you've never confirmed ownership through Google, do that first — an unverified listing can be edited by anyone.
  • Get your category and hours exactly right. The wrong primary category is one of the most common reasons a business doesn't show up for the searches it should.
  • Add real photos. Your storefront, your team, your finished work — not stock images.
  • Ask happy customers for reviews, and reply to every one. A short, genuine reply to both good and bad reviews shows up in front of every future customer who reads that page.
  • Keep it current. Seasonal hours, new services, and posts about what you're doing right now all signal to Google (and to customers) that the business is active.

None of this requires a redesign or a big budget. It requires about twenty minutes and then a habit of checking back in.

Your website and your profile work as a team

Here's the part that's easy to miss: your Google Business Profile and your website aren't competing for the same job — they're two stops on the same path. The profile is what convinces someone in ten seconds that you're legitimate, open, and worth a look. The website is where they go next to see your full range of work, get real pricing context, or fill out a contact form. If either one is missing or neglected, the other has to work twice as hard to make up for it, and a lot of customers won't wait around for that.

For a small business in a competitive local market like Frederick, getting both pieces right — a complete, active Google Business Profile and a fast, professional website that backs it up — is one of the highest-return, lowest-cost things you can do this quarter.
